## 📌 Featured Project

### 🏗️ B2B Marketplace for Reused Construction Materials — Odoo 19 SaaS

A multi-vendor marketplace connecting suppliers and buyers of reclaimed building materials, built entirely on **Odoo 19 Enterprise (Odoo Online)** using Studio, the website editor and server actions — no custom modules, because the SaaS platform does not allow installing addons.

**The constraint that shaped everything:** the specification opened by requesting *"a custom module unique to the platform"*. On Odoo Online, custom modules cannot be installed. Spotting that mismatch — and deciding what the configuration layer could and could not carry — was the central piece of functional work.

**Delivered:**

- **Multi-vendor eCommerce** — catalogue across ~90 public categories, product pages showing avoided CO₂
- **Supplier workspace** — catalogue management, live counters, task-based shortcuts
- **Analytics dashboard** — revenue, product views, add-to-cart, a three-rate conversion funnel, period and category filters
- **Monthly carbon report** — computed against **6,518 ADEME emission factors**, with a declared coverage rate: products without a factor are excluded from the total, never estimated. An unsubstantiated environmental claim is sanctionable under Belgian and EU law.
- **AI material recognition** — photo analysis pre-filling category, condition, dimensions, title and a price estimate, bounded by a reference price table that corrects out-of-range values
- **Dispute management** — helpdesk with 2 teams and 10 stages, both parties notified, e-mail threading keeping every exchange traceable inside the platform
- **Delivery flow** — per-supplier notification, customer confirmation via tokenised link, automatic confirmation after five days

**Scale:** 33 server actions · 15 project QWeb views plus 134 customised native views · 47 custom fields · 9 e-mail templates · 15 website pages · 1 custom model

**Documentation produced:** technical manual, phased rebuild guide and a non-regression fixture set, so the project stays maintainable by someone else.

**What I took from it:** architecting a production application inside SaaS constraints, reading a specification critically instead of executing it literally, and writing documentation good enough that the work survives the person who did it.
